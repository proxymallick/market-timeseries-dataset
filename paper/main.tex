\begin{abstract}
I describe six tables curated from agency publications and a documented crypto-market source. They contain 99,216 rows after counting two formats of the same CFTC data once: 2,465 timestamp-qualified economic events, 4,219 review events, 2,233 hourly Hyperliquid-derived bars, 4,084 Treasury-futures Traders in Financial Futures reports, 3,196 Australian government-yield observations, and 83,019 CFTC disaggregated futures-and-options reports. These tables use different units and clocks, so the total is not an aligned sample or a count of fully timestamp-eligible records. The manifest contains 54 files (106,875,828 bytes; about 106.9 MB). The CFTC product includes an 88 MB CSV and a 16,201,232-byte text-preserving Parquet companion with the same 83,019 rows. My broader inventory found 56,653 physical files occupying 241,884,028,581 bytes, including duplicates, archives, and model or analysis artifacts. I document the schemas, timing, missingness, exclusions, source-rights limits, and known coverage gaps. I report no forecasting experiment or benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and scope}
Financial datasets often mix clocks, units, publication lags, and reuse conditions. Clear documentation of those differences is necessary for responsible reuse, consistent with the framework proposed by Gebru et al.~\cite{gebru2021}. I use this paper as both a dataset description and an audit of the preparation. It records what I retained, what I excluded, and the conditions that limit interpretation.

I brought event-level economic releases together with market and policy series, but I did not harmonize them into one panel. Rows do not share a cadence, instrument universe, unit, or missing-data mechanism. I also kept two event tables for a specific reason: \texttt{economic\_events} contains rows that passed the release-time evidence filter, while \texttt{economic\_events\_review} preserves date-only items and uncertain revisions. The review table is not an intraday event sample.

\section{Preparation and availability}
All counts and schemas below come from the measured snapshot. The 99,216 rows are not unique, aligned, or uniformly timestamped observations. The review table alone contains 4,129 records without a verified release instant, while the other tables are hourly, daily, weekly, or date-only. My filesystem inventory records 56,653 files and 241,884,028,581 bytes. The market category accounts for 236,633,734,490 bytes and includes 27,131 Qlib-related copies. I also found packaged copies, archives, model outputs, and 1,072 verified duplicate groups. These figures describe the files I found, not a number of unique datasets.

The preparation is private and unpublished. It has no DOI or public download. I excluded source material when provenance or redistribution rights remained unresolved. For example, the transformation log records that I removed 577 FDIC rows from the review-event input because I could not establish dataset-specific reuse terms. Public visibility and third-party license labels are not enough to prove redistribution rights.

Both event tables keep identity and revision keys, source provenance, evidence fields, event dates, quality status, optional values, and UTC timestamps where supported. The primary table contains 2,465 typed timestamp rows, all marked `eligible'. In the review table, 4,129 rows have unknown release times and 90 require source-revision review. The primary table contains 1,581 ABS and 884 Federal Reserve observations: 1,581 scheduled-macro, 766 central-bank, and 118 credit/systemic records. These are curation labels, not a taxonomy of the global economy. The review table contains 3,307 AOFM, 323 USDA, 312 Federal Reserve, 240 RBA, and 37 ABS rows. The counts reflect what I assembled and the timestamp filter, not each source's publication rate.

\subsection{Market and policy schemas}
The Hyperliquid Parquet schema contains `item\_id', a timezone-aware timestamp, OHLC prices, volume, amount, and trade count. None of the nine measured columns is null in the 2,233-row snapshot. The upstream order-book dataset has a DataCite record and CC BY 4.0 rights metadata; I retained only a derived hourly aggregation, not the order-status or book-diff corpus~\cite{albers2026,dataciteapi}. One month and three instruments cannot support broad claims about other assets, venues, or periods.

The CFTC TFF table contains instrument identity, observation and availability timestamps, open interest, and long, short, and spread positions for the reported trader groups. All 20 fields are complete in this snapshot. The 191-column disaggregated CSV preserves CFTC names, report categories, positions, changes, percentages, and contract metadata across 543 instruments and 83,019 rows. I verified null counts only for its market-name and report-date fields, not every numeric field. These are weekly reported positions, not transactions or daily holdings. CFTC guidance covers agency-created public-domain information but excepts third-party contributions, so attribution and artifact-level review still matter~\cite{cftctff,cftcterms}.

RBA F2 provides observation and availability timestamps with five percentage-yield series: 2-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year nominal yields and the 10-year indexed yield. The table has 3,196 daily observations on a weekly publication schedule. Missing values total 98 each for 2-, 3-, and 5-year yields, 20 for 10-year nominal, and 413 for 10-year indexed. The RBA's Financial Data terms govern this table separately from the general website license and require attribution without implied endorsement~\cite{rbaf2,rbaterms}.

\section{Time, nulls, and sampling}
The event tables carry a source-local timestamp string, typed UTC fields, an availability time, and an ingestion time. These answer different questions: reference date, publication instant, source clock, and local processing time are not interchangeable. The primary table has no nulls in its typed event and availability timestamps. The review table has 4,129 nulls in each of `event\_timestamp\_local', `event\_timestamp\_utc', and `available\_timestamp'. I left date-only CFTC rows as dates instead of inventing times.

Missingness carries meaning here. The primary event table has 2,395 nulls in both `actual\_value' and `unit'; forecast, prior, revised-prior, surprise, and normalized-surprise are null for all 2,465 rows. This is a release and document ledger where timing can be known without a numeric result. In the review table, 896 rows lack actual values, and all 4,219 lack forecast and prior values. It is not a balanced panel of forecasts and realizations.

\section{Provenance and rights boundaries}
I sourced the tables from Australian and US agencies, CFTC reports, and a documented Hyperliquid-derived file. ABS website material generally uses CC BY 4.0 subject to exclusions. The Federal Reserve says its website information is public domain unless noted otherwise, and CFTC describes agency-created information as public domain while excepting third-party contributions. RBA F2 has separate Financial Data terms. I retained AOFM and USDA material only under the conditions and artifact-level limits recorded in the audit~\cite{absrights,fedrights,aofmrights,usdarecord,nalrights}.

I excluded material with unresolved or incompatible reuse conditions. A Kaggle page labels a Coinbase-derived dataset CC0, while Coinbase's Market Data Terms restrict redistribution and derivatives. That conflict does not settle the rights chain, so I left the corpus out~\cite{coinbasekaggle,coinbaseterms}. I also excluded FI-2010 despite its paper and Fairdata record, Binance Vision because its terms are noncommercial and share-alike, and the Qlib-heavy holdings whose original provider remains unknown~\cite{ntakaris2018,fi2010fairdata,binanceterms}. These are audit decisions, not legal opinions.

\section{Appropriate uses and limitations}
The tables support descriptive work on release-time evidence, weekly CFTC positioning, a short history of three perpetual instruments, and RBA yield coverage. Point-in-time research should use availability timestamps and treat the review table as an uncertainty set, not a confirmed intraday calendar.

This selection does not represent all markets, releases, assets, participants, or periods. The crypto sample covers about one month and three instruments. RBA covers selected maturities. CFTC reports are thresholded weekly aggregates. The event tables have source and timestamp selection bias, and publication calendars can change. Null counts describe one measured copy; they do not prove correctness or the absence of measurement error.

The raw order-book source contains wallet identifiers. I did not include them in the hourly OHLC table, which aggregates by instrument and hour. That statement applies only to this output schema; it is not a general anonymization claim. The data should not be used to link pseudonymous activity to real people or infer sensitive attributes.

\section{Ethics and maintenance}
The measured data snapshot is dated 9 October 2026 and remains unpublished. Before release I will verify authorship details, access location, current source terms, artifact notices, and the intended redistribution scope. A new curation run must regenerate statistics, schemas, inventory, and transformation records from an identified snapshot. Any source or rights change should produce a versioned release and refreshed documentation. I leave the DOI and public download fields absent until they exist.

\section*{Data availability}
The preparation is private and unpublished as of this paper's date. It has no public download or DOI. The manifest contains 54 files (106,875,828 bytes), including an approximately 88 MB CFTC CSV and a 16,201,232-byte text-preserving Parquet copy. They encode the same 83,019 rows and count as one of the six products. Public availability depends on the remaining source-level and artifact-level checks.
